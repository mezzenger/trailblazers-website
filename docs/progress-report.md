**Phase 3A batch:** Ada Lovelace, Grace Hopper, Mae Jemison, Maryam Mirzakhani, Chien-Shiung Wu, Alice Ball, Katalin Karikó, Elizabeth Blackwell, Mary Seacole, Harriet Tubman, Ida B. Wells, Malala Yousafzai, Ellen Johnson Sirleaf, B. R. Ambedkar, Nelson Mandela, Gabriela Mistral, Amrita Sher-Gil, Miriam Makeba, Maya Angelou, and Jocelyn Bell Burnell. Their existing candidate IDs were preserved. Forty-two new citations come from at least two independent domains per person. Two portraits were added after checking their exact file records: NASA photo S92-40463 of Jemison and U.S. Navy photo DN-SC-84-05971 of Hopper. Their creator, government-work license basis, attribution, and notices are recorded in the content. Other new profiles use typographic placeholders.

**Research decisions:** Lovelace's 1843 published procedure is described without an unqualified “first programmer” label. Tubman's birth year is approximate, and the documented count of people she personally guided is distinguished from larger popular totals. The 1974 and 1957 Nobel awards are distinguished from the experimental contributions of Bell Burnell and Wu. Seacole's British Hotel is described as a business and care site, distinct from formal military hospital nursing. Sher-Gil's cause of death is left unspecified because the museum account says it remains uncertain. Sirleaf's postwar presidency is described alongside continuing debate about its reform record. Penn lists Karikó's Ph.D. as 1982 while the University of Szeged lists 1983; her biography says she earned a doctorate without asserting a year. Pages from the UN, U.S. Navy, Smithsonian, the Nobel site, and other hosts that could not be opened were replaced by accessible institutional or scholarly sources; none is relied on as a sole inaccessible citation.
